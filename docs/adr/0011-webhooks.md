# ADR 0011 — Outgoing webhooks

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-25

## Context

SPEC §10 asks for HMAC-signed webhooks for issue, request, approval and comment events, with
retries, exponential backoff and a delivery log. PostgreSQL is the only infrastructure (ADR 0002),
and a slow or broken receiver must never slow down or fail the change that caused the event.

## Decision

- **Outbox in PostgreSQL.** Domain services call a `WebhookEmitter` after their change; it
  writes one `webhook_deliveries` row per matching endpoint and returns. Enqueue errors are
  logged, never raised.
- **Worker delivery.** Worker replicas claim due rows with a short lease
  (`UPDATE … WHERE id IN (SELECT … FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED)`), POST them with a 10 s timeout and
  no redirects, and record the status. Failures retry after 1 min, 5 min, 30 min, 2 h, 6 h, 12 h
  and 24 h, then the delivery is `failed`. Order is kept per endpoint within a batch only;
  receivers should rely on the envelope's `createdAt` and `id`.
- **Signing like Stripe.** `Ordler-Signature: t=<unix>,v1=<hex HMAC-SHA256(secret, "t.body")>`
  plus `Ordler-Event` and `Ordler-Delivery`. The secret (`whsec_…`) is generated by Ordler,
  shown once on create or rotate, and stored sealed with the instance key, because signing
  needs the plain value.
- **Endpoints are organization configuration** (`webhook.manage`), scoped to the
  organization, a department or a team, with an event list. Payloads carry ids, names and URLs,
  not descriptions; request comments state their visibility.
- **Delivery log** keeps 30 days; admins can send a test `ping` and redeliver any entry.
- Internal addresses are allowed: on-premises receivers usually are internal, and only admins
  configure endpoints.

## Consequences

- At-least-once delivery: receivers must deduplicate by the envelope `id`.
- SLA events join the list when SLA timers land.
- Bulk imports (ADR 0010) emit no webhooks, like they send no notifications.

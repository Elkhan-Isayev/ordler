# ADR 0017 — Department wikis with whiteboard diagrams and Wiki.js import

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-12

## Context

Departments keep their knowledge (runbooks, onboarding, policies) in Wiki.js or similar tools
next to Ordler. They asked for it inside Ordler: a space per department with folders and pages,
diagrams drawn with the whiteboard engine (ADR 0015), a way to bring existing Wiki.js content
over, and the same MCP access agents already have for issues.

## Decision

- **Data**: `wiki_folders` (tree by `parent_id`, at most 8 levels), `wiki_pages` (Markdown,
  `version` counter, archived instead of deleted) and `wiki_page_revisions`, one row per saved
  version, append-only. `departments.wiki_visibility` is `department` or `organization`.
  Everything stays in PostgreSQL; search is `ILIKE` over title and text, enough for the
  thousands of pages a department has.
- **Access** reuses RBAC: new permissions `wiki.read` (Viewer and up) and `wiki.edit`
  (Member and up). A grant counts for a department when its scope is the department or any of
  its teams, so team members read their department's knowledge. `department.update` manages
  visibility, deletes any page and imports. The team → department directory is now loaded for
  team-scoped grants too.
- **Lost updates**: edits carry the version they started from; a newer stored version answers
  `409 wiki_conflict`. Simpler than live co-editing and enough for documentation.
- **Diagrams are whiteboards**, not a second engine: a paragraph that is only a link to a
  board embeds the live board. Plain Markdown keeps pages readable in exports, the API and
  MCP; boards keep their team's access, so a reader without it sees a note, not the board.
- **Files** in pages are attachments owned by the department (`attachments.department_id`,
  `team_id` now nullable) and follow the space's read access.
- **Editor**: Tiptap's table and task-list extensions (MIT, same version as the editor) are
  enabled so GFM tables and checklists survive editing — imported pages rely on them.
- **Wiki.js import** (2.x GraphQL API, API key as bearer token, `https` only): pages and path
  prefixes are chosen in the UI; the runner (lease per batch of 10 pages, like ADR 0010)
  rebuilds the folder tree, converts HTML pages with a small converter on `htmlparser2`
  (MIT, already in the dependency tree through `mailparser`), copies linked files from the
  wiki's own origin only, and finally rewrites links between imported pages.
  `external_refs` (kind `wiki_page`) makes re-runs update changed pages and skip the rest;
  pages deleted in Ordler are not brought back. The key is sealed and erased at the end.
- **MCP** gets read tools under `read` and writes under a new token scope `wiki:write`;
  `create_whiteboard` can embed a new diagram straight into a page.

## Consequences

- Concurrent editors of one page get a conflict instead of a merge; live co-editing can be
  added later on the realtime hub.
- Wiki.js comments, page history and per-page permissions are not imported; Ordler's access
  is per department.
- Search is substring-based; PostgreSQL full-text search can replace it if spaces grow large.
